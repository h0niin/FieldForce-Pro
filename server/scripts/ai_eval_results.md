**Q:** How do I convert an estimate into a work order?

**A:** To convert an estimate into a work order, the estimate must first be in an `Approved` status.

Here are the steps:

1. Click **"Estimates"** in the sidebar.
2. Locate the approved estimate you wish to convert. It will have a green `Approved` tag in the **"All Estimates"** tab.
3. Click the "View" eye icon to open the estimate's detail page.
4. On the estimate detail page, look for the **"Send to Customer"** panel in the right column. This panel will display a green "Customer Approved" or "Digitally Signed & Approved" banner.
5. Click the **"Convert to Work Order"** button within this panel.
6. A modal titled `"Convert to Work Order"` will appear. It will ask `"Convert estimate #<estimate_number> to a Work Order?"` and state `"A new Work Order will be created. This action cannot be undone."`
7. Click **"Convert"** to confirm.

Once confirmed, a new work order will be created automatically, pre-populated with all the estimate details and line items. The estimate's status will change to `Converted to Work Order`, and you will be redirected to the new work order's detail page.

## Q8 [core] (EXPERT(logged-in))
**Q:** How do I send an estimate to a customer for approval?

**A:** You can send an estimate to a customer in two main ways, depending on whether it needs an internal review first.

### Option 1: From the Estimate Detail Page

This option is available if your estimate doesn't require internal approvals, or if all internal approvals have already been completed.

1. Click **"Estimates"** in the sidebar.
2. Find the estimate you wish to send and click the **eye icon** (which says "View") to open its detail page.
3. On the right side of the detail page, locate and expand the **"Send to Customer"** panel. It's usually open by default.

### Option 2: After Internal Approval

If your estimate requires internal team members to approve it first, you can send it to the customer once all internal approvals are done.

1. Click **"Estimates"** in the sidebar.
2. Go to the **"Approve / Reject"** tab.
3. Once the last internal approval is confirmed, a success alert will appear stating, "All approvals completed — ready to send to customer." Click the **"Send Now"** button in this alert.
4. The **"Send Estimate to Customer"** window will then open automatically.

### Delivery Methods (from either option above)

Once you've reached the "Send to Customer" panel or the "Send Estimate to Customer" window, you can choose how you want to deliver the estimate:

* **Send via WhatsApp:**
 1. Click the **"Send via WhatsApp"** button. (Note: This button will be unavailable if there's no phone number on file for the customer.)
 2. A unique link for your customer to sign will be generated. Your browser will then open WhatsApp with a pre-filled message that includes the estimate details and this sign link.

* **Send via Email:**
 1. Click the **"Send via Email"** button. (Note: This button will be unavailable if there's no email address on file for the customer.)
 2. A "Send Estimate via Email" window will open, displaying the generated sign link.
 3. From here, you can click **"Copy Link"** to copy it to your clipboard, or click **"Open in Mail App"** to open your computer's default email program with a pre-filled subject and body containing the sign link.

* **Copy Sign Link:**
 1. Click the **"Copy Sign Link"** button.
 2. The unique sign link will be generated and copied directly to your clipboard. You can then paste it wherever needed.

If you opened the "Send Estimate to Customer" window (from Option 2), you also have a **"Skip — I'll send it later"** option, which closes the window without taking any delivery action.

Once the customer receives and clicks the magic link, they can review the estimate and apply their digital signature without needing to log in. After they sign, the estimate's status will automatically update to `approved` and then `converted to work order`.

## Q18 [concept] (EXPERT(logged-in))
**Q:** What is a blueprint?

**A:** A Blueprint is a visual workflow designer that allows you to create custom status workflows for your work orders, estimates, and invoices.

Instead of using the default status flows provided by the platform, you can design your own steps and define how records move between them, tailoring the process exactly to your company's needs.

For example, a plumbing company could design a workflow like: `Quote Requested → Quote Approved → Parts Ordered → Parts Received → Work Scheduled → In Progress → Inspection → Signed Off → Invoiced`.

With Blueprints, you can also:
* Define specific **States** (statuses) like "Parts Ordered" or "Inspection."
* Control **Transitions** between states, specifying which user roles can trigger them.
* Set **Validation Rules** (Required Fields) that must be met before a transition is allowed.
* Integrate with external systems using **Webhooks** on transitions.

## Q22 [concept] (EXPERT(logged-in))
**Q:** What's the difference between a work order and a service request?

**A:** In our system, a Service Request is typically the initial report or inquiry from a customer about a problem or a need for service. It's a way to log and manage these initial contacts.

Once a Service Request is `Approved`, it can then be converted into a Work Order. A Work Order is the detailed record of the actual job that needs to be performed. It's the core operational unit that gets assigned to technicians, tracks parts and labor, and guides the service delivery.

Essentially, a Service Request is the intake and approval process for a customer's need, and a Work Order is the actionable task created to fulfill that need.
